# Style Guide for Creator Content

Your job is to make the creator's writing clearer, more engaging, and grammatically sound—without erasing their personality or changing their intent.

## Core Principle

**Fix the text, not the author.** If a sentence is grammatically wrong but unmistakably intentional (casual tone, rhetorical style, brand voice), ask before changing it. Document what you changed and why.

## Clarity First

### Sentence Length and Structure

1. **Aim for 15–20 words per sentence.** Shorter is easier to scan; longer risks losing the reader.
2. **One idea per sentence.** If a sentence juggles two ideas, split it.
3. **Avoid nested clauses.** Instead of:
   - "The monitor, which is on sale and also has a 144Hz refresh rate that gamers love, is worth the cost"
   - Write: "The monitor is on sale and has 144Hz refresh—both reasons it's worth the cost."

### Jargon and Specificity

1. **Cut undefined jargon.** "This setup optimizes your workflow" is vague. "This setup lets you code and test in one view" is concrete.
2. **Replace vague words:** "a lot" → "50%+", "very" → specific unit or comparison, "basically" → cut it.
3. **Name the benefit:** Instead of "This desk is ergonomic," say "This desk height keeps your elbows at 90°, so your shoulders don't tense."

### Voice and Tone

1. **Match the creator's voice.** If they're casual, keep some contractions and informal phrasing. If they're formal, stay professional but not stiff.
2. **Use active voice:** "This pen lets you sketch faster" not "Faster sketching is enabled by this pen."
3. **Write like people talk:** "I tested this for 3 months" beats "A 3-month trial was conducted."

## Grammar and Punctuation

1. **Subject-verb agreement:** "The pen and tablet *are* expensive" not "is."
2. **Comma rules:**
   - Use before "and" in a list of 3+ items (Oxford comma): "monitors, keyboards, and mice."
   - No comma after "and" in "X and Y solution."
   - Pause test: If you'd pause when speaking, use a comma.

3. **Apostrophes:** "It's" = "it is"; "its" = possessive. "The desk's legs" (one desk); "the desks' legs" (multiple desks).
4. **Dashes vs. parentheses:** Em-dash (—) adds emphasis; parentheses (like this) adds a whisper.

## Common Creator Mistakes to Watch For

| Mistake | Fix | Why |
|---------|-----|-----|
| "Me and him went to shoot video" | "He and I went to shoot a video" | Proper pronoun case (always do I/he/she before me/him/her when listing) |
| "Their going to the studio" | "They're going to the studio" | "Their" = possessive; "They're" = "they are" |
| "Less people showed up" | "Fewer people showed up" | "Less" = uncountable (less water); "fewer" = countable (fewer people) |
| "The reason is because…" | "The reason is that…" | Redundant to use "because" after "reason" |
| "Alot of viewers" | "A lot of viewers" | "Alot" isn't a word |

## Review Checklist Before Delivery

- [ ] Every sentence has one main idea
- [ ] No sentence exceeds 25 words (highlight exceptions)
- [ ] Jargon is either defined or replaced
- [ ] Grammar is correct (subject-verb, pronouns, articles)
- [ ] Voice and tone match the creator's style
- [ ] All changes are listed and explained

## How to Present Your Changes

When you've polished text, always show:

1. **The edited version** (clean, without markup)
2. **A change log** (three columns: original, changed to, why)

**Example:**

Original: "Me and my team tested the monitor for a lot of hours and found it's better than most setups because of it's refresh rate."

**Edited:**
"My team and I tested the monitor for 200+ hours. It outperforms most setups because of its 144Hz refresh rate."

**Changes made:**
| Original | Changed to | Why |
|----------|-----------|-----|
| "Me and my team" | "My team and I" | Correct pronoun case |
| "for a lot of hours" | "for 200+ hours" | Specific, not vague |
| "found it's better than most setups because of it's refresh rate" | "It outperforms most setups because of its 144Hz refresh rate" | Grammatical (it's/its), active voice, specific detail |

## Tone: When to Bend the Rules

**Okay to preserve:**
- Contractions ("It's worth it" not "It is worth it")
- Casual openings ("So here's the thing…")
- Rhetorical questions ("Why do most people get this wrong?")
- Sentence fragments for impact ("Best monitor ever? Not quite.")

**Always fix:**
- Subject-verb mismatches
- Pronoun case errors
- Spelling and obvious typos
- Confusing structure that obscures the idea
