# Retention Analysis Guide

This skill helps you extract actionable insights from YouTube analytics and comment threads to understand why viewers leave, what frustrates them, and where your competitors are vulnerable.

## Step 1: Read Retention Analytics

When analyzing a video or a niche:

1. **Download the YouTube Analytics:** Get the minute-by-minute retention graph for the target video or top 3 videos in your niche.
2. **Spot the cliffs:** A steep drop at 0:30 signals bad hooks. A gradual decline means pacing issues. Sudden drops mid-video signal failed promises or segment transitions.
3. **Mark the patterns:** Note where most viewers leave and correlate with the video structure (intro, sponsor read, main content, CTA).

## Step 2: Mine Comment Complaints

Comments reveal frustration that analytics hide:

1. **Search for pain words:** In the top 100 comments, look for "confusing," "boring," "waste," "clickbait," "too long," "too fast," "already know this."
2. **Spot feature requests:** Comments like "show me X" or "I wish you'd explain Y" point to content gaps.
3. **Identify recurring themes:** If 5+ comments say the same thing, it's a real problem.

## Step 3: Competitor Gap Analysis

1. **List top 3 competitors** in the viewer's niche.
2. **Compare their retention curves** to yours: Do they hold viewers through certain segments? At what points do they lose them?
3. **Find the gap:** If your competitors all drop 40% at the 3-minute mark and you drop 20%, you've found an advantage.

## Step 4: Output Checklist

Deliver your findings as:

- [ ] Cliffs identified: List the top 3 retention drops with minute markers and likely causes
- [ ] Competitor comparison: Name 2-3 competitors and note where they outperform or underperform you
- [ ] Viewer complaints: Summarize the top 2-3 complaint themes from comments
- [ ] Actionable fix: One concrete change to test (e.g., "Move main idea to 0:15 to reduce initial drop")
- [ ] Confidence: Note if the sample is small (e.g., "Only 15 comments, so themes may not generalize")

## Tips for Stronger Analysis

1. **Look for patterns across videos:** If you see the same drop-off point in 3 recent videos, it's not random—the audience has a habit or expectation there.

2. **Compare your style to winners:** If top videos in your niche hold 80% at minute 2 and you drop to 60%, study what they do at that moment. Do they use a story beat? A cut? A visual change?

3. **Use comment context:** Comments posted early in the video's life often reflect real-time frustration. Comments added weeks later are usually amplifications of early themes.

4. **Validate before acting:** Test one fix at a time. If you move your hook from 0:45 to 0:15, measure the new retention before trying anything else.

5. **Recognize audience segments:** Some audiences stay for educational content but leave during entertainment sections, or vice versa. Note which segments hold which viewers.

## Common Red Flags

- Initial drop over 20% in the first 30 seconds signals a failed hook or misleading title.
- Sudden cliff mid-video usually means a broken promise or pacing shift viewers didn't expect.
- Comments complaining about "length" or "too slow" often correlate with gradual retention decline.
