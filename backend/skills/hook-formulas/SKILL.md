# Hook Formulas for Video Intros

Your first 5 seconds determine whether viewers stay or click away. These formulas deliver tension, curiosity, or value in the opening moments.

## Formula 1: The Problem Hook

**Pattern:** State a problem your audience faces in the first 2 seconds, then hint at the solution.

**Example (Desk Setups):**
"Most people buy an expensive monitor arm and still end up with back pain. I found why—and it cost $12 to fix."

**Why it works:** Viewers stop scrolling if the video solves their pain. The specificity (back pain, $12) creates credibility.

## Formula 2: The Counter-Intuitive Flip

**Pattern:** Contradict a common belief in your niche, then promise the real truth.

**Example (Art Gear):**
"Everyone says you need a $500 tablet to learn digital art. I proved them wrong with a $40 pen and paper."

**Why it works:** Disagreement with conventional wisdom makes viewers curious to see the evidence.

## Formula 3: The Deadline/Scarcity Hook

**Pattern:** Create urgency without being urgent. Mention a time limit, price point, or stock status.

**Example (Desk Setup):**
"This monitor was on sale for 36 hours. Here's why it beats the $400 option everyone buys."

**Why it works:** Scarcity triggers attention, and comparison justifies why this video is worth your time now.

## Formula 4: The "Show, Don't Tell" Hook

**Pattern:** Open with a stunning visual or transformation. One sentence of context, then show the result.

**Example (Art Gear):**
*[Cut to: a messy desk setup instantly transforming into a clean workstation]*
"90 seconds to a desk that actually boosts your creativity. Here's the before."

**Why it works:** Movement keeps attention. People want to know how the magic happened.

## Formula 5: The List Hook

**Pattern:** Promise a specific, bounded list (3 ways, 5 mistakes, etc.). Name the first one immediately.

**Example (Desk Setups):**
"Three desk setups that actually reduce eye strain. The first one fixes your monitor height—that alone fixes 60% of problems."

**Why it works:** Lists feel like guided tours. Viewers know what they're getting and why the first item matters.

## Formula 6: The Personal Stake Hook

**Pattern:** Mention a specific outcome you achieved or a mistake you made, then invite viewers into the story.

**Example (Art Gear):**
"I wasted $800 on art tablets before I realized what I was actually doing wrong. If you're stuck, this saved me months of frustration."

**Why it works:** Relatability + skin in the game. Viewers trust people who've failed and learned.

## Formula 7: The Direct Question Hook

**Pattern:** Ask a question that nails the viewer's situation, not a generic question.

**Example (Desk Setups):**
"Is your monitor at eye level right now? Probably not—and that's costing you 2 hours of productivity every week."

**Why it works:** Specific questions feel personal. Viewers mentally answer "yes" and want to hear the fix.

## Formula 8: The Credential + Promise Hook

**Pattern:** Establish credibility in one sentence, then state the benefit.

**Example (Art Gear):**
"I've tested 40 drawing tablets in the last two years. The best one isn't the most expensive—and I'm showing you why."

**Why it works:** Authority + value proposition. Viewers know you've done the work and the video will be worth their time.

## Hook Rules: What NOT to Do

1. **No Clickbait:** Every promise in the hook must be 100% delivered in the video. If you say "This art pen changed my life," show measurable improvement or genuine enthusiasm, not exaggeration.

2. **No Fake Urgency:** Don't say something is "limited time" if it's always available. Don't say "shocking secret" if it's common knowledge.

3. **No Misleading B-roll:** The first visual must match the hook. If you hook with a $40 tablet, show the $40 tablet immediately—not a $500 alternative for comparison later.

4. **No Hollow Superlatives:** Avoid "This is the BEST desk ever" without context. Instead: "This is the best desk for [specific use case] because [specific reason]."

5. **Specificity Wins:** "How to set up a desk" is generic. "How to set up a desk so you stop leaning into your monitor" is memorable and click-worthy.
